  1. The record claims more than the test shows about the momentum variables.

  But domain_shift.py trains on all 32 inputs, including HT and all three
  fat-jet pT values. We read that in the code, and Addendum A confirms it. The
  cross-slice test never dropped the momentum variables, so it cannot say
  anything about the "no pT, no HT" configuration. The 0.936 that appears in
  both tables is a coincidence, as Addendum A itself notes. A referee who checks
  the code will catch this, and the dropped-momentum claim is the one point
  Major Comment 8 asks about.
  Next test: rerun the cross-slice test with the 28-input set from
  robustness_check.py (no HT, no j{k}_pT) in both directions. If the 100 → 50
  loss stays near 0.2, the v3 sentence becomes true. If it shrinks a lot, the
  claim has to change.

  2. Why the AUC drops is inferred, not shown. Moderate to serious.
  The current explanation is that the model learns "softer leading jet and lower
  MET mean signal", and the pT>50 background extrapolates into that region.
  That comes from my reading of the sample means: signal j1_pT 127.7 sits
  between Z pT>100 (145.1) and Z pT>50 (87.1), and MET falls to 42 GeV in pT>50.
  HT, multiplicity and subleading masses pull the other way. No measurement
  links the AUC loss to particular features. What we did establish is that the
  pT>50 slice is not intrinsically hard: trained and tested on itself, it
  reaches 0.9095. So the loss is a training mismatch, but which features cause
  it is untested. If the paper names a cause, a referee can ask for evidence.
  Next test: take the pT>100 model and compare its scores on the pT>50
  background with its scores on the pT>100 background, binned in j1_pT and MET.
  If the mismatch is concentrated at low j1_pT and MET, the mechanism holds. A
  cheaper alternative is to permute one feature at a time in the shifted test
  set and see which ones recover the AUC.

  3. Each result is a single split with no uncertainty. Moderate, mainly a 
  presentation issue.
  Table 5 comes from one 70/30 split and one seed, with 6,000 events per class
  in each test set. We corrected the record's "3,000 per class" in this session.
  No error bars are given. As a rough estimate, the statistical error on an AUC
  near 0.73 with 6,000 + 6,000 events is about ±0.005, and smaller near 0.93.
  That is far below the 0.209 drop and well below the 0.043 drop, so I don't
  expect either result to disappear. But that is an estimate, not a measurement,
  and this paper reports cross-validated errors everywhere else (Table 7).
  Next test: five-fold cross-validation of all four cells of Table 5, reporting
  the mean ± standard deviation of each AUC and of both drops.

  Not in the top three: the 14% signal reuse.
  We measured it directly: 843 of 6,000 signal events (14.05%) in one direction
  and 829 of 6,000 (13.82%) in the other. It's real, but it can only make the
  shifted AUC look better, so the true loss is at least as large as reported. It
  works against the claim, not for it. Point 3's cross-validation can fix it at
  the same time by drawing the signal from disjoint pools.
